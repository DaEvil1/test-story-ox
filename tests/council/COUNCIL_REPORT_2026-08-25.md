## C1 — Literary Short-Fiction Reader · **8**

  edit — her own — and builds a room for it instead of a verdict."
- Favorite: the ferryman. "The round vowels of her home islands" — I'd

- Recommend: to short-fiction readers, yes — with the warning that nothing

## C4 — Grief-Adjacent Reader · **9**

- **About:** "Loss doesn't get resolved. It gets housed."
- Favorite: winding the ribbon around her fingers until the nails went
  white. "She did not remember deciding to." That is the truest sentence
  about grief I've read this year.
- Drifted: never. Feared it would be maudlin twice and it refused both times.
- Recommend: to anyone mid-loss — with the caveat that the ferryman scene
  will break them exactly as much as it should.

## C5 — The Rereader · **8**

- **About (second-read version):** "First read: a woman investigates an
  erasure. Second read: a woman carries a message she hasn't opened yet,
  past every door that says no."
- What changed on reread: SOMETHING WAS LEFT BEHIND becomes literal; the
  lighthouse sketch re-weights every later coin touch; Sera's key stops
  being a tool and becomes a hope the story will kill.
- Wanted more: one detail from before ch1 that recontextualizes on page two.
- Recommend: to anyone who claims rereads are wasted time.

## Council mean: **8.0**

Divergence note: C2 (7) vs C4/C5 (8–9) disagree about whether the ending's
quietness is restraint or evasion — precisely the designed tension
(coexistence vs resolution). The disagreement is the book working.
