# Tests: Ira Nash Characterization

Ira is central to Keji's dilemma but absent from most scenes. This test validates that Ira feels like a real person with coherent motivations, not just a plot device.

## Ira's Core Elements

1. **Presence through absence:** Ira is dead/gone/folded away, but their voice and choices shape every scene. The test is whether readers feel Ira's weight despite their absence.

2. **Motivation complexity:** Ira's decision to erase Lio came from a place of genuine (if misguided) care. Readers should understand this without excusing it.

3. **Intimacy with Keji:** Their history is personal, romantic, trusting. Keji's conflict should feel like a betrayal of someone she loved.

4. **The Limit's echo:** Sometimes The Limit speaks in Ira's cadence. Readers should feel uncertain whether they're hearing Ira's logic or The Limit's, or whether there's a difference.

## Validation Checklist

- [ ] Ira's voice appears on recordings/fragments with distinct tone and word choice.
- [ ] When Keji encounters Ira's logic, she has a visceral reaction (tightened chest, breath held, hands trembling).
- [ ] At least one scene where Ari alludes to Ira's impossible position (showing sympathy without absolution).
- [ ] The reader should feel Ira's love for Keji and Lio *and* their willingness to erase one of them.
- [ ] In the final chapter, Ira's absence should feel significant. Their non-appearance should mean something.
- [ ] Readers should not fully know whether Ira is alive, dead, or folded into the Archive itself.
