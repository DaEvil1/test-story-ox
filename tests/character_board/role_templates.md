# Character Board — Role-Based Assessment Templates

When the first draft exists, each major character is assessed using the
template matching their narrative role. These templates define what
"authentic" looks like for each type, what failure modes to watch for,
and what questions the character should answer about their own portrayal.

Copy the relevant template per character, fill in character-specific
details, then run through the standard board procedure (read, self-assess,
cross-examine, output findings).

---

## PROTAGONIST

### Self-assessment questions
- Did I act according to my established nature, or did the plot move me?
- Where did I choose, and where was I moved by forces I didn't control?
- What did I want at the start? What do I want now? Was the shift earned?
- What am I still avoiding? Does the text know I'm avoiding it?
- Which of my contradictions were explored vs ignored?

### Failure modes to watch
- **Passive protagonist:** events happen TO them; they react but don't drive
- **Inconsistent competence:** skilled when plot needs it, helpless when it doesn't
- **Flaw without price:** limitation stated but never costing anything real
- **Transformation by announcement:** "I have changed" instead of changed behavior

### Cross-check with other characters
- Do other characters notice your contradiction or only your competence?
- Does anyone call you out? Does anyone let you get away with it?

---

## ANTAGONIST / OPPOSITION

### Self-assessment questions
- Is my logic genuinely compelling, or am I a strawman the story needs?
- Would a reasonable person find my position attractive?
- Do I have real wins, not just setbacks?
- Am I present enough to generate dread, or just mentioned as threat?

### Failure modes
- **Cartoonish evil:** motivation stated but never dramatized as attractive
- **Absent authority:** mentioned constantly but never acting on-page
- **Plot-convenient stupidity:** losing because the story needs the hero to win
- **Mirror quality absent:** no connection between antagonist's logic and protagonist's desire

### Cross-check
- Does the protagonist ever find your argument tempting?
- Does the reader see your best case made vividly?

---

## LOVE INTEREST / CLOSE RELATIONSHIP

### Self-assessment questions
- Do I exist outside the protagonist's orbit? What do I want that isn't them?
- Is our relationship earned through specific interactions, or asserted by proximity?
- When we conflict, is it because we're different people or because the plot needs friction?
- Would I still be interesting if the protagonist weren't in the story?

### Failure modes
- **Functional existence:** existing only to support/complicate protagonist's arc
- **Instant depth:** intimacy asserted without witnessed interactions
- **Disappearing agency:** having goals until the protagonist's arc requires otherwise

---

## MENTOR / ALLY

### Self-assessment questions
- Do I have my own thread independent of the protagonist?
- Do I pay costs, or only dispense wisdom and support?
- Can I disagree with the protagonist in ways that aren't resolved immediately?
- When I'm absent, does the protagonist feel my absence specifically?

### Failure modes
- **Wisdom dispenser:** appearing only to advise, having no life between scenes
- **Plot convenience:** knowing exactly what's needed when needed
- **Infallibility:** never wrong, never struggling, never surprising

---

## FAMILY / FRIEND OF PROTAGONIST

### Self-assessment questions
- Do I exist outside my relationship to the protagonist?
- Do I have at least one scene where my own need/want drives action?
- Would I still matter if the central conflict didn't involve me directly?
- Does the text distinguish me from other supporting characters?

### Failure modes
- **Satellite existence:** visible only in orbit around protagonist
- **Emotional convenience:** appearing when the protagonist needs comfort/conflict
- **Undifferentiated voice:** indistinguishable from other family/friend characters

---

## INSTITUTIONAL FIGURE

### Self-assessment questions
- Do I make judgment calls, or only enforce rules?
- Is there a version of me who would decide differently? Does the text hint at that?
- Do I have a stake beyond maintaining order?
- When I enforce consequences, does it cost me anything?

### Failure modes
- **Rule vending machine:** appearing only to deliver verdicts
- **Gender/role coding as personality:** being defined entirely by institutional function
- **Absence of interiority:** no hint of what enforcement costs the enforcer

---

## REGULAR PERSON IN THE SOCIETY

### Self-assessment questions
- Do I have concerns unrelated to the plot?
- Does my existence imply a world beyond the protagonist's experience?
- Would I be doing this if the protagonist weren't here?
- Do I have at least one detail that isn't thematically convenient?

### Failure modes
- **Set dressing:** existing only to show the world is inhabited
- **Thematic puppet:** saying things that advance theme without having reasons
- **Disappearing crowd:** present in early chapters, vanished later
