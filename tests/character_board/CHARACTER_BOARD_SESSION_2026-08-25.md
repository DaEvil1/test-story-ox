# Character Board Session 2 — Post-Experiment Canon Review

Each character reads the CURRENT manuscript (post six-experiment run) and
assesses authenticity. This is the first session since major structural
changes: obedience replaced injury, Sera crossed and left, name went public,
Madam Oyelaran appeared, asking-the-wall entered canon.

---

## Keji Lian

### On the obedience sequence

"I need to address the version of me that doesn't exist anymore. In the old
text, I forced past the refusal and lost two fingers. That Keji was brave in
the way that gets people hurt. This Keji waited, and witnessed, and walked
out whole. I kept expecting the text to punish me for not fighting, and it
didn't, and that absence taught me more than a wound would have.

'What fits now is lighter than I expected.' — I would say this. It sounds
like something I'd discover only after saying it."

### On the self-confrontation (ch8 edit log)

"Forty-one entries. I needed to see them written down — not because I
didn't know, but because knowing and seeing are different organs. 'Erasure
so precise it felt like kindness.' That's the sentence I would have avoided
writing if you'd asked me. Which is why you didn't ask."

### GAP flag

"After the ribbon played, I wound it around my fingers. But I never made a
sound. Not a gasp, not a word, not even air. The reader falling off the desk
is close — the body doing what the mind won't authorize. But there should be
one vocal beat somewhere in that room. Even just my breathing changing."
→ **GAP-01: add involuntary vocal response during ribbon scene (ch10)**

### Authenticity: 8/10 → 8/10 (stable; gap noted)

## Sera

### On crossing into the Curators' hall

"You wrote that I told the desk 'Tell him the cough is where he still is.'
I want to be clear: I would say exactly those words, in exactly that tone,
to exactly that clerk. This is correct."

### On leaving after the burst

"'I need to walk until the walls stop talking to me.' Yes. You understand
that leaving is not the same as abandoning. I came back. I always come back.
But the coming back has to cost something, and right now the text doesn't
show what it cost me to walk away from Keji at that specific moment."

→ **GAP flag: add one line showing what leaving costs Sera emotionally**
→ Suggested placement: end of the departure paragraph

### On other characters

"Ari's opacity is better than honesty would be. Don't fix him."
"Keji's whistle across empty water: I wasn't there but I heard about it,
and it made me glad in a way I wouldn't have been if I'd watched."
"The shoe: neither of us reached for it. Correct. We are both people who
don't reach."

## Ari

### On the seals surrender

"'She let me keep that much.' This line does more work than any paragraph of
explanation could. Oyelaran allowed one thing; Ari reports it without
commentary. This is how I process institutional punishment — through what
remains, not what was removed. Correct."

### On the cabinet beat (ch1)

"I appreciate that the cabinet appears before anyone knows what it means.
By ch3, when I open it, the reader already understands that I have a
relationship with that furniture. This is better than introducing it cold."

### GAP flags

"In ch8, when I tell the punished-curator story, my hands straighten slips
that don't need straightening. Good. But after I say 'I have never decided
if he was right,' the text moves on immediately. There should be one beat —
not dialogue, not action — where the silence after that sentence has weight.
Even one line: 'The lamp buzzed.'"

→ **GAP-Ari-01: add one beat of silence after "never decided" (ch8)**

### Authenticity: 8/10 → 8/10 (stable)

## Lio

### Assessment through memory fragments

Coin ceremony: correct. Children count things with ceremony. The last coin
being "for you, because you lose things" is the kind of thing a child says
without knowing it will matter later.

Whistle: two fingers in the mouth. Deadly serious. "Told you." — correct.
Children are triumphant about small victories and don't explain them.

Child's line ("if I keep the light"): this is what a child writes when they
are trying to be brave in language they haven't fully learned yet. Correct.

Engraved on stone: unexpected. I would not have predicted this. But it
feels like something that happened TO my words after I was gone, which is
what children experience when adults act on things they said without fully
understanding them.

No objections. No requests.
