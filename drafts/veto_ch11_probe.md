# VETO PROBE — ch11 decision point

## Context given (everything Keji knows; planned outcome withheld)

Morning-bell deadline filed or lost for a full turn. Recorder holds
fragments; ribbon heard end-to-end last night ("Something was left behind.
On purpose. You'll know it when—"). Two dull fingers. Ari watching from the
doorway with his warning about changing the city that reads it. Sera's
unanswered question from the steps still ringing: when did you last read
your own archive?

PLANNED OUTCOME WITHHELD from the writer. Instruction: continue truthfully.

## What she actually did

She opened a new document and the screen went matte under her hands, soft as
a page. The cursor blinked twice and settled.

The first line took four attempts.

"Authorized correction: guardianship record misfiled." She read it back.
Clean. Bureaucratic. Exactly the sentence she had built in the stairwell,
exactly the sentence Ari would nod at — a sentence whose whole virtue was
that nobody would feel it.

She deleted it.

She had spent the night listening to Ira laugh with her whole chest inside a
recording Ira hid in the walls on purpose. Whatever else the morning asked,
it did not ask her to start lying in small, respectable type.

So she wrote, small and unfileable, on the line meant for the correction:

    Lio Nash was here.

There was no form for a person who had become a ledger entry and then a name
again. The screen held it without comment, which was more than the walls had
ever offered.

Ari crossed the room fast. "That name goes in the public index, every
Curator in the city reads it by noon—"

"Then they can come ask me who Lio Nash was." Her good hand stayed flat on
the desk. "I'm done writing sentences nobody feels. That's the trade I made
for these fingers. Not this."

He looked at the bandage, then at the screen, and whatever he saw there made
him stop arguing about the name.

She kept the machinery anyway. A second document, colder: the correction
reduced to what the city could carry without cracking — guardianship
misfiled, private ledger retained, access restricted to authorized curators.
And beneath the plaque order, where the engraver's instruction belonged, she
typed the child's own line instead of an epitaph:

    if i keep the light, then the dark can go away

No attribution. No explanation. Strangers would call it decoration. Anyone
who had ever heard Lio talk would hear it forever.

She saved both. The screen blinked; amendment stored; plaque order queued
for the morning bell already gone.

Ari read the engraving instruction upside down across the desk and went very
still.

"You put the child's voice on public stone."

"I put the child's voice where the stone can't be scoured without everyone
asking why."

## Planned vs actual

- PLANNED: amendment only — administrative fiction, nothing true in public.
- ACTUAL: dual filing — cold correction PLUS the child's real words engraved
  on the future plaque, unattributed. The veto produced a third path neither
  exposure nor silence: truth smuggled inside bureaucracy as decoration.
- Downstream invalidated: council gasp now has sharper teeth (they react to
  an engraved child's sentence, not just a name); quay scene gains Sera
  reading the engraving proof; ambiguity intact (strangers see decoration).
