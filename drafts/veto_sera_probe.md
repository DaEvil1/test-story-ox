## What happened

Sera walked into the Curators' hall on the third morning and asked the desk
for Ari by name.

The clerk looked at her bare wrists — no curator's band, no visitor's mark,
Drift dust still on her sleeves — and asked her to state her business for
the index. She said: "Tell him the cough is where he still is." And she sat
down on the marble bench to wait, because Sera does not queue, she installs
herself.

Ari came down in four minutes. Whatever he expected from the Drift, it was
not her. They looked at each other across twenty years of separate
decisions — he had signed things; she had refused things; both of them had
lost people to the difference.

"You don't belong under this roof," he said.

"Neither does the ledger she's keeping," Sera said. "But there it is, one
floor below you. I want to see the room where she hides the true thing.
I want to know if it smells like courage or like fear."

Ari did not answer that. But he did not call the desk either. He walked her
upstairs himself, past the seals, past the lamp, and stood in the doorway
of the private ledger room while Sera read Keji's card aloud, once, in a
neutral voice, the way you test whether a floor holds weight.

Then she left without touching anything.

Keji found out an hour later, from the terminal — which greeted HER by name
and then, for the first time, added a second line: TWO VISITORS LOGGED.
