## What is genuinely good

1. **Keji's agency and the amendment ending (#10, #36)** — a protagonist
   whose victory is authorship, resolved by the story's own logic. This is
   the novella's spine and it works.
2. **Theme fully enacted (#21, #24, #28)** — show-don't-tell discipline is
   real; the prose rules were fought for and it shows.
3. **The image system (#24)** — memory as touchable material, walls as
   readable text: concrete, original, load-bearing.
4. **The Limit's concept (#14, #16)** — a boundary-force wearing a loved
   one's cadence is a strong antagonist idea, felt everywhere.

## What holds it back (priority order)

1. **No lived relationship (#12, #33, #34)** — insert 1–2 brief specific
   memories of Lio *alive* and Keji-with-Ira *warm* (flashback, artifact
   sequence, or tidepool-style fragment). Grief needs witnesses.
2. **Costless opposition and climax (#15, #23, #37, #32)** — give the Limit
   one real extraction: a failed read that seals something forever, Ari
   paying a price, the city visibly reacting to the amendment. One cost
   would lift four criteria.
3. **Middle repetition (#4, #5, #7)** — differentiate ch7/ch9/ch10's scene
   values or merge two; make the midpoint flip alter Keji's behavior.
4. **Emotional monotone (#8, #26, #33)** — permit one warm, loud, or joyful
   sequence (a market scene, a remembered tidepool) for contrast.
5. **Thin central ambiguity (#19)** — seed evidence for two readings of
   Ira's act (mercy *and* betrayal) so the unanswered question weighs.

## Honest bottom line

This is controlled, coherent, professional work — roughly a 6 at its best
moments, a 4 in its structural middle, never careless. Its flaws are almost
all *omissions* (unshown relationships, unpaid costs, unvaried register)
rather than mistakes, which makes them unusually fixable in revision. As it
stands it respects its reader and its own rules; it does not yet risk enough
to break anyone's heart.
