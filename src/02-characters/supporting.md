# Supporting Characters

## Ari
- Role: Mentor and senior curator
- Core belief: Order is compassion when the archive is fragile.
- Contradiction: Ari edits memories to protect people from pain, but this creates hollow loyalties.
- Why they matter to the theme: Ari represents the seductive safety of curated memory.

## Sera
- Role: Drift catalyst and disruptive friend
- Core belief: Chaos in memory preserves authenticity.
- Contradiction: Sera is fiercely free, but she runs from the consequences of the Archive's noise.
- Why they matter to the theme: Sera challenges Keji to accept the Archive's wildness.

## Ira Nash
- Role: Keji's former partner and an influential curator during the erased event
- Core belief: Sometimes memory must be shaped for the greater good.
- Contradiction: Ira cared for Keji deeply, but supported or enacted a decision that led to painful erasure.
- Why they matter to the theme: Ira's choices are the personal axis of Keji's dilemma — restoring what Ira helped hide could condemn a past intimacy or reveal a necessary truth.


## The Limit
- Role: Opposite of Keji's eventual choice, appearing indirectly at first and sometimes voiced as echoes of Ira.
- Core belief: Reality must be constrained to stay coherent; uncontrolled memory leads to collapse.
- Contradiction: The Limit may not be a person at all, but a pattern in the Archive that hems everything back into order; however, its most persuasive moments use the cadence of Ira's voice.
- Why they matter to the theme: The Limit tests whether Keji's acceptance of chaos is courage or delusion; its tether to Ira makes the choice painfully personal.
- Narrative function: Its influence appears as strange reminders in the city, odd lines in archived logs, whispered prompts that sound like Ira, and as a question in the minds of other characters.

## The Archive
- Role: Setting and quasi-character
- Core belief: It wants to balance order and entropy.
- Contradiction: Its acts of preservation are also acts of omission.
- Why it matters to the theme: The Archive embodies the question of whether reality should be fixed or fluid.
