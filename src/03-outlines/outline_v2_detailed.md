# Detailed Outline v2

## Story Overview

Keji Lian is a memory specialist in the Archive, a city built around a living reservoir of remembered experience. Her work is to make memory tidy, but a persistent glitch leads her toward a hidden event and a peripheral force called The Limit. As the Archive itself seems to whisper, Keji must decide whether a self made of edited traces is still human.

## Act 1: The Fault Line

1. Keji is introduced at work in the Archive's editing room. The prose shows memory as tactile: threads pulled, inked fragments reconfigured, breath held in data pulses.
2. A glitch appears in Keji’s own archive: a line of text she never wrote, a corridor that will not stop rearranging, and an echo on her palm from a voice that is not hers.
3. In the market of living walls, Keji finds a vanished message painted in a language no one claims.
4. At the Curators' hall, Ari explains the Archive's purpose and warns that deep layers are sealed for a reason. His care is clear, but so is his fear.
5. Keji meets Sera in the Drift, where living memory spills into the streets. Sera argues that some fragments must be allowed to run wild. She mentions a hidden boundary called The Limit, a line the city refuses to cross.
6. Keji decides to investigate the anomaly, stepping into a world of memory that resists being neatly categorized.

## Act 2: The Hidden Layer

7. Keji moves into the Archive’s hidden strata. The architecture shifts: stairs that become riverbeds, rooms that replay old arguments as if they were weather.
8. She finds a preserved fragment of the erased event: a public vow, a ritual, an emergency that was wiped clean. The fragment is imperfect, full of gaps and implied fear.
9. The Limit's influence appears indirectly: a wall that keeps resetting itself, a piece of data that vanishes when Keji tries to write it down, and other citizens who suddenly remember a rule that never existed.
10. Keji returns to Ari with the fragment. He insists the old event is dangerous and that some forgetting is part of survival.
11. Sera offers another path: use the Drift to experience the memory as it wants to be felt, not only as a restored record.
12. Keji struggles with her own archive resisting the probe. Her glitch grows stranger: it now feels like a presence watching her decisions.
13. She chooses to access the memory through the Drift, risking an unstable merge between her curated self and the wild Archive.

## Act 3: The Limit

14. Keji reaches the erased core, a space where memory is raw and the city’s past is still warm. The Limit becomes more direct, either as a voice in the walls or a figure at the edge of perception.
15. She confronts the central question: does the Archive need coherence to survive, or does the self need mess to be honest?
16. The Limit argues for a constrained reality, claiming that untamed memory will tear the Archive apart and leave people lost.
17. Keji feels the weight of both philosophies. She chooses to carry the imperfect self, allowing some chaos back into the Archive rather than sealing it behind a perfect narrative.
18. The Archive shifts in response, the city stuttering between order and drift.
19. In the aftermath, Keji accepts that some things are unresolved. She keeps The Limit's nature ambiguous, and the story ends with her deciding how memory will be lived rather than merely edited.
