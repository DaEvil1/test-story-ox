Chapter 7 — The Table of Light

The wall in the Drift had been quiet when Keji first touched it. Now it thrummed under her fingertips like a thing with weight, and it did not like being read.

Sera watched the letters turn beneath her hand. The line was thin and reluctant, spilling out only once before closing on itself.

"...Lio will become a ledger entry. The present will be preserved..."

The word "ledger" landed in Keji's spine like cold metal.

Sera slid the wire away from the wall. "Won't say more unless you ask it in the right place."

Her fingers stayed on the surface. "Then show me the right place."

The Drift's passage tilted. The air cooled; ink lifted off the wall and formed a path. It curved toward a door hidden beneath a layer of old script.

Only a faint seam marked the door. When her palm met it, the surface pulsed and the hidden line changed. A single word appeared, slow and heavy: NO.

Under her palm the wall was ridged, like a surface built out of many erased attempts.

She pushed once and the door held, but a pressure rose behind it—a force not in the door but in the air around it.

"The Limit is here," Sera murmured. "It resists with the first word."

She pushed again. The door's seam widened a hair, and on the wall the letters rearranged into a second word: FURTHER.

Not a sentence yet. Just a pair of words. The message was raw. The door's refusal was a boundary.

Behind the door, a sound began to stir: the scrape of a chair, the rustle of paper, the faint exhale of someone holding their breath.

She did not let the sound distract her. She traced the seam with the edge of her palm, feeling the door's pulse. Behind it, pressure shifted; the hidden line changed once more.

"...Lio must be carried privately..."

The sentence stopped. The wall went silent. The door did not open.

Sera's hand came to Keji's shoulder. "It is not asking for permission. It is telling you where to stop."

She stepped back, knee hitting the wall, and the stone carried a cold tremor as if it had been charged.

She reached into her pocket and closed her hand around the coin. Private sat in her mind, heavy as the metal itself.

"Then we go to the room itself," she said.

Sera did not argue. She led the way down a passage that narrowed until the air itself felt thicker, walls layered with old recorder plates, each humming at its own pitch, the low notes reverberating in Keji's chest.

At the end of the passage a chamber opened, unlit—what light there was came up from beneath the floor, from the recordings themselves. The air tasted of rain and dust and the sharp edge of paper that had been folded too many times.

In the center of the chamber was a table. Not a physical table, but a surface of paused light. Faces moved there only as shifting pattern—no detail, only motion like what she remembered from the Archive's old viewing rooms.

The memory began in fragments. A hand placing a paper on the surface. A voice saying the word AUTHORIZED. Another voice answering with a soft, insistent no.

Her breath shortened. The sound was not exactly in the room. It was in her bones, through the floor.

She leaned closer. The light on the surface formed a line of text that slid past too fast to read. She followed it with her mind. The first clear thing was not a full sentence, only a decision dressed as a command: "REMOVE CHANNEL."

In the light, the faces changed. A new voice came through, steadier. It said the name 

"Lio."

The name came differently than the others, spoken in a way that carried someone with it.

Keji's throat tightened. Around her the chamber reacted: floor vibrating, walls holding back a pulse like a tide held by a dam.

Then the voice spoke again—the cadence she had heard before, but clearer.

"We preserve the network. The channel closes. The child becomes ledger. Necessary."

The memory did not soften or justify them. It said them as if the choice had been made and was being put in place.

Then the table's surface flickered, and another line formed below the first: 

"NO FURTHER."

The Limit had spoken in a human rhythm. It did not ask. It ordered. The second line was not a plea. It was a boundary.

She pressed her palm to the table of light. The air around her thickened. The surface blurred. She could feel the record wanting to close.

She reached for the coin in her pocket and set it on the table's edge. The metal touched the surface. The light around it shifted. The coin did not belong to the record, but it made the contact real.

A new fragment rose out of the pulse. It was thinner, younger. It said, "...if I keep the light, then the dark can go away..."

The voice was a whisper of a child's thought.

The room convulsed. The wall on her right bowed inward and then snapped back. The hidden line on the table pulsed with a final message.

"KEEP PRIVATE." 

The chamber exhaled.

She drew her hand back to a warm coin as the table of light dimmed and the faces in the memory receded.

Sera did not move. Her breath came slowly. "It gave you one line it wanted to keep."

Coin back in pocket, its metallic edge pressing under her fingers. "It also showed me the door."

Sera's gaze followed the passage back. "Then the next step is not to open it. It's to decide what we carry."

Keji nodded once.

When they climbed back into the Drift, market noise unchanged. But she felt the hidden door inside her now, a weight that was not visible to anyone else.
